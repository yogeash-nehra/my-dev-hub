## Benchmarks & Leaderboards

### [HIGH] Claude Opus 5 Benchmark Consolidation: Frontier-Bench #1 (43.3%), ARC-AGI-3 SOTA (30.2%), SWE-bench Pro 79.2%

**Source:** SWE-bench Pro Leaderboard / ARC Prize (official) / Anthropic launch materials | **Date:** July 25, 2026 (SWE-bench Pro leaderboard entry; ARC Prize official X post July 24) | **Link:** https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks/swe-bench-pro

**What changed:** Claude Opus 5's SWE-bench Pro entry appeared July 25 (79.2%), completing the benchmark picture after the July 24 launch. Across four key developer benchmarks, Opus 5 leads the frontier on reasoning and agentic coding while matching Fable 5 on software engineering tasks — at half the price.

**TL;DR:** Claude Opus 5 scores 43.3% on Frontier-Bench (agentic coding, leads all models), 30.2% on ARC-AGI-3 (>3× the prior record of 7.8% set by GPT-5.6 Sol), 96% on SWE-bench Verified, and 79.2% on SWE-bench Pro (vs Fable 5 at 80.3%) — at $5/$25 per MTok vs Fable 5's $10/$50.

**Developer signal:** Four benchmarks, one clear takeaway: Opus 5 is optimized for agency and reasoning, not general-purpose breadth. (1) **Frontier-Bench v0.1** (74-task agentic coding benchmark, successor to Terminal-Bench 2.1): Opus 5 at 43.3% max effort leads GPT-5.6 Sol (37.5%) and Fable 5 (33.7%) by a 6–10 point margin. If you are building agentic coding pipelines or computer-use workflows, Opus 5 at $5/$25 is now the primary option to benchmark — it leads the frontier and costs half of Fable 5. (2) **ARC-AGI-3** (novel reasoning; no memorization possible by design): Opus 5 at 30.2% (High effort) is more than three times the previous best of 7.8% by GPT-5.6 Sol running at Max effort. ARC Prize confirmed it solved five previously unbeaten environments. The caveat: ARC-AGI-3 was evaluated at High effort only, not Max, due to the short testing window — the Max-effort score is not yet available. (3) **SWE-bench Pro** (harder version of SWE-bench Verified; leaderboard updated July 25): Opus 5 at 79.2% is third behind Mythos 5 (80.3%) and Fable 5 (80.0%), and ahead of its predecessor Opus 4.8 (69.2%) by a full 10 percentage points. The Fable 5 vs Opus 5 gap on SWE-bench Pro (1.1 pp) is narrower than on Frontier-Bench (9.6 pp in Opus 5's favor) — suggesting different optimization targets. (4) **SWE-bench Verified**: Opus 5 at 96%, Mythos 5 at 95.5%, Fable 5 at 95% — the three Anthropic models cluster at the top. Practical guidance: for long-running agentic coding agents and computer-use tasks, switch to Opus 5; for knowledge-work breadth, Fable 5 remains the default. The price differential ($5/$25 vs $10/$50) is now decisively in favor of Opus 5 for agentic use cases.

**Affects you if:** You are choosing between Claude Opus 5 and Claude Fable 5 for an agentic coding, computer-use, or reasoning-heavy workload; you are benchmarking models for production agent selection.

**Adoption effort:** Quick — Opus 5 is a drop-in swap from Fable 5 (`claude-fable-5` → `claude-opus-5`); pricing favors Opus 5 for agentic workloads. Verify output quality on your specific task distribution before committing.

**Primary source:** ARC Prize official X post (July 24): https://x.com/arcprize/status/2080716561539907928 | SWE-bench Pro leaderboard: https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks/swe-bench-pro | Frontier-Bench: https://codersera.com/blog/claude-opus-5-benchmarks-explained-2026/

### Kimi K3 Weights Land in Under 24 Hours — vLLM KDA Support Shipping Alongside

**Source:** Moonshot AI (official Hugging Face org) | **Date:** July 26, 2026 | **Link:** https://huggingface.co/moonshotai

**What's happening:** Kimi K3 open weights drop July 27, 00:00 UTC (about 16 hours from this digest). New confirmed detail not in yesterday's coverage: vLLM support for KDA (Kimi Delta Attention — the hybrid linear/full attention architecture) and prefix caching ships alongside the weights. The technical report publishes simultaneously. Weight footprint: ~1.4TB in MXFP4 before runtime buffers. Architecture: 2.8T total / ~50B active (16 of 896 experts per token). License: Modified-MIT (fine-tuning and redistribution permitted). The model has been available via the Kimi API and kimi.com since July 16 (World AI Conference in Shanghai).

**Why watch this:** The vLLM KDA support is the key infrastructure unlock. Without it, inference would require custom CUDA kernels; with it, teams can run K3 on vLLM v0.26.0+ immediately after weights land. The practical constraint remains the hardware floor: ~1.4TB MXFP4 memory before KV cache, requiring Blackwell cluster access or significant CPU offloading. The simultaneous technical report is worth reading for KDA performance vs standard full attention at production context lengths (50K–200K tokens), since the 1M-context claim is the architectural ceiling, not typical workload distribution. If the report includes short-context quality data, that's the number that determines whether K3 competes with API-tier models at inference costs most teams can afford.

**[TENSION] Kimi K3 is simultaneously the most open and the least accessible frontier model**
Kimi K3 ships under a Modified-MIT license — fine-tuning permitted, redistribution permitted, weights public. By the letter of "open weights," it is the most liberally licensed frontier-class model ever released. By the practicalities of infrastructure, it is accessible only to hyperscalers and large research labs: ~1.4TB MXFP4 before runtime buffers, Blackwell-optimized, requiring what amounts to a 16+ H200 or equivalent Blackwell cluster just to load. The vLLM KDA support (confirmed today) lowers the software barrier to near zero for teams with the hardware. The hardware barrier, however, is not falling: Kimi K3 raises the floor by roughly 2× compared to the previous largest open-weight releases. "Open weights" is becoming a legally meaningful but practically tiered designation. A model can be MIT-licensed and effectively closed to 99% of practitioners simultaneously — the license matters for governance and compliance, not self-hosting economics for most teams.
*Grounded in: Kimi K3 1.4TB MXFP4 footprint (July 25 digest, Trends); Modified-MIT license (July 25 digest, Trends); vLLM KDA support confirmed (this digest, Trends)*

**[BUILDER'S ANGLE] ARC-AGI-3 at 30.2% unlocks a new class of self-improving test generation**
ARC-AGI-3's defining property is that it cannot be solved by memorization — each task requires genuine novel reasoning. A model that scores 30.2% on it (solving tasks no prior model could solve, including Fable 5) has demonstrated the ability to generalize to held-out problem structures. The builder's angle: if Opus 5 can reliably solve ~30% of ARC-AGI-3 problems it has never seen, it can potentially generate *new* test problems at that same difficulty level — creating evaluation tasks that previous models couldn't pass. This unlocks a compound eval loop: Opus 5 generates hard test cases, validates them against itself, and produces a difficulty-calibrated test suite that automatically challenges models at the frontier. No one has published this pipeline yet (as of this digest), but the capability precondition (a model that can solve novel, non-memorizable reasoning tasks at scale) is now met.
